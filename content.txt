Medical Insurance Cost Prediction & Risk Segmentation
Context: IS 507 - Data, Statistical Models & Information, UIUC (Fall 2025)
Team: 5 members
Dataset: 1,338 records — Medical Insurance Cost Dataset (Kaggle)
Tech Stack: Python (Scikit-Learn, Pandas, NumPy), R, Random Forest, K-Means, GAM, Regression
Research Questions
	•	RQ1: How do age, BMI, and children influence charges — do nonlinear patterns amplify costs?
	•	RQ2: Does smoking amplify the effects of age and BMI on medical charges?
	•	RQ3: Do insurance charges differ by U.S. region after controlling for demographics?
	•	RQ4: Can insurance charges be accurately predicted from demographic and lifestyle factors?
	•	RQ5: Can clustering identify distinct health-risk groups differing in average charges?
Key Findings & Metrics
	•	Random Forest: R-squared = 0.82, MAE = $2,746 — best performance across all models.
	•	Smoking is dominant cost driver (feature importance = 0.41), nearly double age (0.233) and BMI (0.218).
	•	Significant BMI x Smoking multiplicative interaction (p < 0.05).
	•	K-Means clustering identified 3 distinct health-risk segments with clear cost separation.
	•	Regional differences disappeared after controlling for individual risk factors.
